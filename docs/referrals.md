## 2. Product decisions and functional scope

### Proposed launch policy v1.0

The following terms were supplied as **proposals**, expressly subject to subscription-margin validation before activation. They are not authorization to activate rewards or make them available to customers. Store an immutable policy version and configure future versions administratively; do not hardcode commercial terms into services. Resolve the remaining questions below before publishing the policy.

| Area | Proposed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Participants | Any registered user with verified email, active account and no referral restrictions may refer; a paid subscription is not required. Referred users must be genuinely new, with no prior Simora account. Self-referrals and multiple accounts for rewards are prohibited. |
| Attribution | First valid link wins for 30 days from first capture; later links do not replace it while valid. A new valid capture may be made after expiry. Codes remain valid while the referrer is eligible. Revalidate at registration, including referrer suspension; lock attribution to the new account. Existing users cannot be newly attributed. Restricted administrative fraud corrections require an audit trail. |
| Qualification | First successful, verified paid subscription within 90 days after registration; a payment after the 30-day capture window can qualify if registration locked attribution in time. Registrations, free activation, deposits and betting activity do not qualify. One qualification per referred customer; renewals and upgrades do not create additional rewards. |
| Referred customer | 10% discount on the first subscription purchase, applied immediately at checkout under a server-generated quote. |
| Referrer | One-time non-withdrawable subscription credit equal to 20% of the actual first subscription payment, capped at ₦2,000; eligible for release 14 days after verified payment, subject to review of qualifying status. No recurring commission and no fixed referral-count cap initially; anti-abuse controls apply. |
| Use and reversal | Credit applies only to eligible future Simora subscription invoices; no withdrawal, transfer or betting balance. Cancel pending rewards for refunded, reversed, fraudulent or charged-back payments. Preserve an auditable ledger and idempotent processing. Administrators can review statistics, liabilities, suspicious activity and controlled adjustments. |

Illustration supplied for policy validation: a ₦5,000 subscription with a ₦500 discount results in a ₦4,500 payment and a ₦900 referrer credit before processing fees and taxes. This is an example, not a catalog price or proof of margin. Store percentages, caps, timing and eligibility in the published policy version; retain the applicable version for existing relationships and quotes. A secure, first-party HttpOnly cookie backed by server-side attribution, subject to applicable consent, preserves the provisional capture; validate it again server-side at registration. The 30-day capture lifetime and 90-day qualification deadline are distinct.

**Remaining launch decisions:** confirm margin validation and publication authority; whether the discount applies to all confirmed plans, taxes, partial-credit purchases and first invoices with zero payable balance; define rounding and the payment amount used when tax, fees, credits, refunds or partial payments exist; whether a partially refunded payment cancels the entire pending reward; treatment and recovery of credit already released when a payment is later reversed; precise payment verification and 14/90-day boundary semantics; credit expiry, consumption order, partial use and treatment on account closure; duplicate-account evidence, review and appeals; identity changes and manually corrected attribution; privacy consent, retention and terms; administrator permissions and adjustment approvals. The user-provided pending-reward cancellation rule does not specify recovery after release. Do not implement a debit or negative balance until that policy and the billing ledger are approved.

**Decisions required from product and operations:** validate and approve the proposed policy above, program owner and launch/terms; campaign overlap; code rotation and manual-code precedence; cross-device handling and consent; detailed subscription/payment and refund/dispute treatment; rounding, credit expiration, settlement accounting and reconciliation; fraud thresholds and appeals; account suspension/deletion and retention; permissions, approval thresholds and support access; disclosures, tax and privacy review; metric definitions and reporting timezone. Confirm whether anonymous landing attribution is permitted and how consent applies. The v1.0 values are proposals, not active configuration.

Capability acceptance is conditional on those decisions:

1. Eligible authenticated participants can retrieve a stable code and canonical HTTPS link, share it, and view published terms. Server-issued codes are opaque and cannot embed a user identifier. A disabled, expired or revoked code cannot create new attribution; existing relationships remain historically intact subject to policy.
2. A visitor may present a code through the registration journey. Capture is provisional, server validated, time bounded by approved policy, and attached at most once at the authoritative account-creation boundary. Invalid codes never block ordinary registration unless explicitly approved; show a safe explanation and allow removal. Cross-device recovery is not promised without an approved mechanism.
3. A relationship progresses independently from qualification and reward settlement. A qualifying event must originate in a trusted backend module; clients cannot claim conversions, amounts, status or another user's referral. Qualification evaluates the versioned rules applicable to that relationship.
4. Only an approved reward definition creates a reward obligation; settlement, redemption, expiry, reversal and recurring rewards remain unavailable until their respective models and operators are approved. No fictional balance or claim button is shown. Manual review cannot bypass eligibility or ledger invariants.
5. Participants see only their own activity and authorized aggregates. Administrators operate within separately granted permissions; actions and decisions are auditable. Program suspension stops new actions as configured, with treatment of existing relationships and obligations explicitly determined before activation.

## 3. Lifecycle, attribution and failure handling

Proposed relationship states: `pending` (registered, awaiting qualification), `qualified`, `disqualified`, `expired` and `reversed` (previously qualified but authoritative event invalidated). Allowed transitions: pending → qualified/disqualified/expired; qualified → reversed; reversed → qualified only on an explicitly approved reinstatement and a new authoritative event. Terminal state corrections require an audited decision, not direct status editing. Each transition records reason, rule version, event, effective time and actor/system. Pending means a registered relationship, not an anonymous visit. An unregistered capture expires separately and never appears as a referral.

Proposed reward states, conditional on settlement approval: `pending_review` → `approved`/`rejected`; `approved` → `settlement_pending` → `settled`; eligible pending/approved obligations → `cancelled`; settled → `reversal_pending` → `reversed` only after verified compensation. Failed or unknown external settlement stays pending for reconciliation. Store reversals as linked new entries, never delete original entries. Whether review is mandatory and what counts as settled depend on approved rules; no transition implies automatic payout. If partial reversals or non-money benefits are approved, define their units and accounting separately before implementation.

1. An authorized participant retrieves an existing active code or requests creation through a CSRF-protected idempotent command. Generate using a cryptographically secure random source; unique index and retry collisions. Link uses configured trusted origin, not request Host; encode code and enforce HTTPS.
2. Landing or explicit entry submits the code to server validation. If permitted, create a random, opaque, signed or server-backed short-lived attribution token in a Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookie. Keep only the minimum needed, no third-party trackers; do not log full tokens. Existing attribution is replaced only under approved precedence rules. Validation does not reserve a reward or reveal the referrer's identity.
3. Registration server validates token/code, program version, time, eligibility and referrer/account status again. Under a single DB transaction create the user and relationship, consume the capture once and emit an outbox event; registration must remain correct if attribution is absent or invalid. Bind an explicit code to the registration request only after server validation. A unique referred-user constraint prevents competing referrers; lock capture and recheck. Reject known self-referral by user ID and route suspected duplicate identities to review; identity signals are not proof of fraud.
4. Trusted registration, account verification, subscription and verified payment/adjustment events reach the referral consumer via a transactional outbox. Verify event provenance, stable event ID and referenced authoritative records; deduplicate in an inbox, lock relationship, evaluate the immutable applicable rule version and record decision and outbox in the same transaction. Event order can vary: fetch current authoritative state, defer missing dependencies, and re-evaluate on relevant changes. Never trust browser redirects, request parameters, unverified webhooks or fixture data.
5. If rules authorize a reward, calculate from trusted snapshots using integer minor units for money and approved rounding. Uniquely identify the obligation by relationship, reward rule, beneficiary and approved occurrence/period. Issue once under a DB uniqueness constraint and lock; recurring periods require explicit policy. Settlement delegates to the approved billing/ledger provider boundary using a stable idempotency key; unknown results require reconciliation before retry. Never create an independent spendable wallet by assumption.
6. Refunds, chargebacks or subscription reversals are new authoritative events. Record the decision and applicable compensation, with policy specifying timing, partial amounts and negative-balance handling. Concurrent qualification, reversal and admin commands lock the same relationship/reward and use version checks. No external call inside the transaction.

Duplicate link visits create no duplicate relationship; concurrent registrations resolve by unique constraint and capture consumption. Invalid/expired/revoked codes are rejected without identity disclosure; paused campaigns apply approved grandfathering rules. A referred account cannot refer itself; a later merged/duplicate account requires an audited review and explicit correction. Account deletion must follow an approved retention/anonymization policy: revoke new use, remove direct personal data as permitted, retain restricted minimal obligations and audit records where legally required; avoid cascades that erase financial history. Decide effects of suspension before enabling. Queue failure retries with backoff, deduplication, dead-letter alert and replay; recovery uses recorded event IDs and reconciles source records. No privacy retention period is assumed.

## 4. Architecture and persistence

Use thin MVC controllers and validators, injected clock and repositories, with `ReferralCodeService` (issue/validate), `AttributionService` (capture/consume), `ReferralService` (relationship/transitions), `QualificationService` (trusted evidence/rule evaluation), `RewardService` (obligation, not settlement), `ReferralAdminService` (authorized review/configuration), and analytics read service. A settlement adapter delegates only to an approved billing/ledger service. Auth owns users and registration; billing owns payments/subscriptions and their truth; notifications consume referral events without owning referral state. Reuse shared audit, outbox, queue, identity, authorization and rate limiter once available. Services depend on contracts; controllers do not calculate rewards. Do not introduce a separate broker or microservice absent measured need.

Proposed MySQL/InnoDB schema, finalized against the actual user/billing schema and migration conventions. Internal BIGINT keys, opaque public IDs, UTC `DATETIME(6)`, FKs, explicit allowed states, creation/update timestamps and row versions where mutated. Restrict deletion of referenced financial/audit records; policy determines nullable/anonymized user references. Monetary values are integers with explicit currency, never floats. Sensitive rule snapshots and events have least-privilege access.

| Table | Core fields and constraints / access indexes |
| --- | --- |
| `referral_programs` | ID, status, current version, activation bounds; lock/version on changes. Status draft/active/suspended/retired; publication and outstanding-obligation treatment require policy. |
| `referral_program_versions` | program FK, version, effective bounds, immutable eligibility/qualification/reward/terms snapshots and published timestamp; unique `(program_id, version)`. Validate non-overlap under program lock; drafts editable, published versions immutable. |
| `referral_codes` | public ID, owner FK, program FK, normalized random code, status, issued/revoked/expires timestamps; unique code, index owner/program/status. Enforce one active code per owner/program with a locked guard row or generated nullable unique key, if approved. |
| `referral_attributions` | opaque token hash, code FK, program version FK, captured/expires/consumed timestamps, optional consumed user FK; unique token hash, index expiry. No raw token, IP history or unnecessary fingerprint; purge under policy. |
| `referral_relationships` | public ID, referrer and referred user FKs, code/version FKs, state, registered/qualified timestamps, version; unique referred user for proposed single attribution, check distinct user IDs; index `(referrer_id, registered_at, id)` and `(state, registered_at)`. Change uniqueness only if overlapping programs are approved. |
| `referral_transitions` | relationship FK, from/to, reason, rule version, source event ID, actor, effective/recorded time; index relationship/time; append-only. |
| `referral_qualification_decisions` | relationship/version FKs, evidence type/reference, outcome/reason, evaluated time; unique `(relationship_id, source_system, source_event_id, rule_version_id)`, index outcome/time. Capture immutable references/snapshots, not raw payment data. |
| `referral_rewards` | public ID, relationship/rule version/beneficiary FKs, occurrence key, approved type/value snapshot, state, version, expiry if approved; unique `(relationship_id, rule_version_id, beneficiary_id, occurrence_key)`, index beneficiary/state/time. Do not instantiate monetary fields without approved reward definition. |
| `referral_reward_entries` | reward FK, operation, amount/unit/currency, source and linked prior entry, settlement reference and time; unique `(reward_id, operation_key)`; append-only. Actual accounting and partial reversal constraints require approved ledger contract. |
| Shared `inbox`, `outbox`, `audit_events` | unique source/event and event key; durable state, lease/retry and correlation; audit actor, permission, target, reason, before/after references and time. Index pending/lease and target/time. Share platform implementations, avoid parallel infrastructure. |

Indexes support owner-scoped keyset history and worker scans; never derive balances by unsafe concurrent read-modify-write. Lock in consistent order: relationship then reward; enforce uniqueness in DB, handle duplicate-key races as idempotent replay. Capture and registration use a transaction on the same database; if identity is external, design an explicit recoverable saga and compensation before launch. Use forward versioned migrations after users and shared infrastructure, expand/contract deployment, backups and restore rehearsal; test fresh install and upgrade, FK/index constraints and production-sized plans. Do not migrate demo fixtures. Once financial history exists, forward-correct instead of destructive rollback.

## 5. Proposed REST contracts

All endpoints are **unimplemented**. Customer reads require session authentication and ownership except public validation/rules; admin routes require explicit server-side permission. State-changing session requests require CSRF and origin checks. HTTPS; `Cache-Control: private, no-store` for user/admin data. New responses follow `{ "data": ..., "error": null, "meta": ... }`; error is `{ "data": null, "error": {"code":"...","message":"..."}, "meta": {"request_id":"..."} }`. Existing prediction API remains unchanged. IDs opaque, times ISO 8601 UTC, money `{amount_minor,currency}` only after approval. Invalid JSON/parameters: 400; unauthenticated 401; forbidden/CSRF 403; inaccessible object 404; version/idempotency conflict 409; semantically invalid 422; rate limit 429 with `Retry-After`; dependency unavailable 503. Errors are generic and never disclose account existence, code owner, internal stack or secrets. Bound request bodies, page size, filter enums and cursor lifetime; per-IP validation/signup and per-account reads/writes use shared limits configured and tuned before launch, with monitoring and accessibility of legitimate users.

| Method / route | Request and access | Success `data` and special behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GET `/api/referrals/program` | Public; no parameters; expose only approved published rules and terms. | 200 `{status, terms_url, rules_version, explanation}`; absent/inactive is explicit. Cache only non-personal published content if allowed. |
| POST `/api/referrals/code` | Participant + CSRF; empty JSON, `Idempotency-Key`; eligibility checked server-side. | 200 existing or 201 created `{code, link, status, expires_at}`; key scoped to user/operation with canonical request hash. |
| GET `/api/referrals/code` | Participant; no parameters. | 200 `{code,link,status,expires_at}` or `null`; no creation on GET. |
| POST `/api/referrals/validate` | Public; `{code}` bounded normalized string; anti-abuse and same-origin controls. | 200 `{valid, message}` with generic invalid result; no owner identity; no attribution or reward. |
| POST `/api/referrals/attribution` | Anonymous session + CSRF; `{code}` or approved signed landing flow; precedence/consent checked. | 201/200 `{captured:true, expires_at}` and HttpOnly token cookie; invalid/expired returns generic 422, rate-limited. Same request replay returns same capture; never accept referrer user ID. Registration consumes token internally, not via a public conversion endpoint. |
| GET `/api/referrals/overview` | Participant; no client user ID. | 200 `{total, pending, qualified, conversion_rate, rewards}`; rewards null until enabled; definitions below. |
| GET `/api/referrals/history` | Participant; optional `status`, `sort`, opaque `cursor`, bounded `limit`, approved date filters and privacy-safe search. | 200 array `{id,registered_at,status,qualified_at,reward_summary}`; `meta:{next_cursor,limit}`. Only own rows; sort allowlisted and stable `(time,id)`; no referred user's email/name by default. |
| GET `/api/referrals/rewards` | Participant; optional state, cursor, limit; only if reward model enabled. | 200 array `{id,referral_id,state,value,created_at,expires_at}` with cursor metadata; value/type from approved model. |
| GET `/api/referrals/rewards/{id}/entries` | Owner; cursor/limit. | 200 array `{operation,state,value,occurred_at}`; inaccessible ID 404; no provider secrets. |
| GET `/api/admin/referrals/programs`, `/relationships`, `/rewards`, `/audit` | Distinct config/view/reward/audit permissions; allowlisted filters, date range, cursor and bounded limit. | 200 arrays with `meta.next_cursor`; only necessary restricted fields; audited sensitive access per policy. |
| POST `/api/admin/referrals/programs` and `/programs/{id}/versions` | Config-edit permission, CSRF, idempotency; validated complete draft and expected version on edit. | 201 draft `{id,version,status}`; no publication or reward issuance. |
| POST `/api/admin/referrals/versions/{id}/publish` and `/programs/{id}/suspend` | Publish/suspend permission, CSRF, idempotency, `{expected_version,reason}` and policy-required approval. | 200 `{id,status,version}`; 409 stale/conflicting effective window; append audit. |
| POST `/api/admin/referrals/relationships/{id}/review` | Review permission, CSRF, idempotency; `{decision,reason,expected_version}` restricted to approved review transitions. | 200 `{id,status,version}` or 409; no arbitrary status override; audit evidence and actor. |
| POST `/api/admin/referrals/rewards/{id}/review` | Reward-review permission, CSRF, idempotency; `{decision,reason,expected_version}`; require second approval if policy says so. | 200 reviewed or 202 settlement pending; 409 stale; no settlement until approved integration. |

Use bounded idempotency keys with unique `(actor/session, operation, key)`, canonical payload hash, stored outcome and retention exceeding client retry/reconciliation windows. Same key and payload returns original response; different payload gives 409. For concurrent different keys, row lock plus expected version and invariant constraints determine the winner. Admin and settlement writes require the same guarantees. Example success for history: `{"data":[{"id":"opaque","registered_at":"2026-01-01T00:00:00Z","status":"pending","qualified_at":null,"reward_summary":null}],"error":null,"meta":{"next_cursor":null,"limit":20}}`. Examples illustrate shape only, not real records or policy.

## 7. Security, operations and administration

Use secure sessions, CSRF, HTTPS, authorization on every object, parameterized queries, server validation and escaped output. Generate high-entropy codes and tokens, use generic validation responses, shared rate limits and monitored signup velocity; do not rely on obscurity or IP as identity. Anomaly signals (velocity, repeated payment instrument or device where lawfully available) flag review, not automatic guilt. Set thresholds, retention, appeals and staff access by policy. Never log codes/tokens, credentials, payment data or unnecessary PII. Protect audits against alteration and restrict exports. Admin permissions separate configuration, publishing, relationship review, reward review, settlement and audit access; dual control is policy-gated. Audit approvals, changes, reversals and reasons with correlation IDs.

Instrument aggregate validation failures, capture/registration/qualification conversion, duplicate suppression, queue age/retries/dead letters, reconciliation differences and settlement unknowns without personal data in metrics. Define numerical SLOs and alert thresholds with operations before release. Reconcile source events and reward obligations periodically using durable checkpoints and overlapping windows; investigate missing and duplicate events, not silently adjust accounting. Feature flags stop new capture/issuance independently of processing existing obligations; document ownership, incident response, replay, restore and disable behavior. Scheduled expiry and re-evaluation use an injected clock, locked state and idempotent operations. The scheduler must never invent qualifying evidence.

Rewards and administration | Only after decisions: immutable obligations, settlement adapter/reconciliation, reward history; permissions, program publishing, review and audit interface. | Approved ledger/provider, policy and operator roles; idempotency, reversal, accounting, privacy and admin tests pass; no unapproved payout or balance. |
| 7. Launch readiness | CI, end-to-end tests, threat review, load measurement, monitoring, incident/runbook, restore and staged rollout with kill switches. | All enabled phases and owners; §8 passes, outstanding decisions keep dependent features disabled, release sign-off recorded. |

**Design trade-offs:** opaque server-held attribution is more reliable than client-only tracking but depends on session/cookie policy and does not guarantee cross-device continuity. A unique referred-user constraint makes attribution unambiguous but must be revisited if multi-campaign policy changes. Immutable rule versions and outbox/inbox add storage and operational work but preserve decisions and make retries safe. A ledger adapter avoids inventing wallet semantics but intentionally blocks reward settlement until its owner and accounting are defined.
